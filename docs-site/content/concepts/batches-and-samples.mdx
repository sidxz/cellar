# Batches & samples

**What you'll learn:** How chemcellar separates the abstract identity of a compound from its physical
incarnations — and why that separation matters for inventory tracking, screening, and compliance.

---

## The two-level model: molecule → batch → sample

In chemcellar, physical material is tracked at two levels beneath the compound record:

| Level | What it is | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| **[Molecule](/reference/glossary#molecule)** | The abstract chemical entity — structure, name, registration number | CV-00123 |
| **[Batch](/reference/glossary#batch)** | A specific physical preparation of that molecule | CV-00123-001 |
| **[Sample](/reference/glossary#sample)** | A discrete container of material from a batch | Vial barcoded `TBX-00441` |

Every batch belongs to exactly one molecule. Every sample belongs to exactly one batch.
This chain lets you trace any tube in your freezer all the way back to its source compound and the
scientist who made or procured it.

---

## Batches

A **batch** (sometimes called a lot or preparation) represents a specific physical preparation of a
molecule. Each time a compound is synthesized, purchased, or received as a donation, a new batch is
created.

### What a batch records

- **Batch number** — an immutable identifier assigned at creation, formatted as
  `<registration-number>-<sequence>` (for example, `CV-00123-001`, `CV-00123-002`).
  Batch numbers never change, even if the molecule is later merged with another record, so external
  lab notebooks and external system references remain valid.
- **Source** — how the material was obtained: synthesized in-house, purchased from a supplier,
  donated, or extracted from a natural source.
- **Salt form** — for example, hydrochloride, sodium salt, or free base.
- **Purity** — percentage purity as measured (e.g., by HPLC or NMR).
- **Amount & concentration** — the quantity recorded at creation (solid in mg/g, solution in
  mL/L, or molar amount in µmol) and, if the material is in solution, the starting concentration.
- **Storage conditions** — temperature, humidity, and light sensitivity requirements.
- **Supplier information** — vendor name, catalog number, and lot number for purchased material.
- **Chemist** — the user who synthesized or received the batch.
- **Synthesis date and expiry date.**

<Callout type="info">
Batch numbers are immutable. Once assigned, a batch number (`CV-00123-001`) stays fixed for the
lifetime of the record — even if the parent molecule is merged with another compound.
</Callout>

### Why multiple batches?

A single compound may have several batches for legitimate scientific reasons:

- **Different salt forms** — the free base and its hydrochloride salt are the same molecule
  but different physical preparations.
- **Purity differences** — a crude synthesis batch (85% purity) and a purified batch (>99%)
  warrant separate tracking.
- **Different suppliers** — vendor lots may have different impurity profiles.
- **Separate synthesis campaigns** — a scale-up batch and an original discovery batch.

Having distinct batch records lets you know precisely which material was used in a given assay
well — critical for troubleshooting unexpected activity data.

## Samples

A **sample** is a discrete physical container of material drawn from a batch — the unit of day-to-day
inventory management. Think of it as a single vial, tube, plate well, or ampule.

### What a sample records

- **Barcode** — a 1D or 2D barcode that uniquely identifies the container across the workspace.
  Samples are always located by barcode, never by UUID.
- **Container type** — vial, tube, plate well, ampule, or bag.
- **Current amount** — updated each time material is consumed (aliquoted). Amount is always
  non-negative; when it reaches zero the sample transitions automatically to **depleted**.
- **Concentration and solvent** — if the sample is in solution (e.g., 10 mM in DMSO).
- **Storage location** — where in the lab the container physically lives (see
  [storage locations](#storage-locations) below).
- **Status** — one of `available`, `depleted`, `expired`, `quarantined`, or `disposed`.
- **Freeze-thaw count** — incremented each time the sample is thawed; relevant for stability-sensitive
  compounds.
- **Low-stock threshold** — an optional alert level; chemcellar notifies you when the amount drops
  below this value.

### Sample lifecycle

Samples follow a defined lifecycle:

```
available ──[amount = 0]──► depleted
available ──[past expiry]──► expired
available ──[QC issue]───► quarantined
available ──[manual]─────► disposed
quarantined ──[cleared]──► available
quarantined ──[manual]───► disposed
expired ─────[manual]────► disposed
```

`depleted` and `disposed` are terminal states — no further material changes are possible.
`quarantined` is recoverable: a QC administrator can clear the hold and return the sample to
`available`.

<Callout type="warning">
Disposing of a sample is irreversible. Once a sample is marked `disposed`, it cannot be
un-disposed. This action is recorded in the append-only audit trail.
</Callout>

## Amount & concentration value objects

chemcellar tracks quantities using two precise value types rather than bare numbers.

### Amount

An **[amount](/reference/glossary#amount)** is a mass, volume, or molar quantity paired with its unit:

| Unit | Typical use |
|------|-------------|
| mg, g, kg | Solid material |
| mL, L | Liquid volume |
| µmol | Molar quantity |

Amount values are always non-negative. chemcellar does not automatically convert between units
(for example, it will not convert 1000 mg to 1 g) — the unit you record is the unit that appears
in all reports and exports.

### Concentration

A **[concentration](/reference/glossary#concentration)** is a solution concentration paired with its unit:

| Unit | Typical use |
|------|-------------|
| mM | Stock solutions |
| µM | Working solutions |
| nM | Ultra-dilute solutions |
| mg/mL | Protein or formulation concentrations |

Concentration must be strictly positive (zero concentration means no compound is present — that
would be an empty container, not a sample).

## Storage locations

chemcellar represents your lab's physical storage as a hierarchy that mirrors real infrastructure:

```
Site → Building → Room → Freezer / Refrigerator → Shelf → Rack → Box → Position
```

Every sample can be assigned a position in this hierarchy. The storage browser lets you navigate
the tree and see which samples are at each location. Each storage node can optionally record a
temperature (e.g., −80 °C, −20 °C, 4 °C, room temperature) and a barcode.

## Requesting material

When you need material for an experiment, you submit a **[sample request](/reference/glossary#sample-request)** rather
than directly consuming a sample. The request workflow:

1. You specify which molecule (and optionally which batch) you need, plus the amount and purpose.
2. The sample management team reviews the request and locates suitable available samples.
3. They fulfil the request by aliquoting from an available sample, updating its amount, and
   recording which sample was used.
4. The request closes with a link back to the fulfilling sample — giving you a complete audit trail
   from experiment back to physical tube.

<Callout type="info">
This action is recorded in the append-only audit trail.
</Callout>
