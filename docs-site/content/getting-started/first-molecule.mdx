# Register your first molecule

**Who this is for:** Bench chemists and anyone with the `cellar:register` permission who wants to add a new compound to the registry.

**What you'll accomplish:** You will draw or paste a chemical structure, fill in the required metadata, and confirm that chemcellar has created a permanent registration record with a unique registration number.

## Before you start

- You must be logged in to chemcellar and have selected your workspace. See [Logging in](/getting-started/logging-in) if you haven't done this yet.
- Your workspace role must include the `cellar:register` permission. If you see no **Register** button, ask your administrator.
- Have your compound's name and SMILES (or its drawn structure) ready. If you have a CAS number, vendor ID, or other external identifier, have those to hand as well.

<Callout type="info">A [molecule](/reference/glossary#molecule) in chemcellar is a unique chemical structure, deduplicated by its [InChIKey](/reference/glossary#inchikey). If the same structure has already been registered, chemcellar creates a new [batch](/reference/glossary#batch) under the existing molecule record rather than duplicating it. You do not need to check for duplicates manually — chemcellar handles this automatically.</Callout>

---

## What is a molecule vs. a batch?

Before you register, it helps to understand two core concepts:

- A **molecule** is the chemical identity — the structure, the name, the permanent registration number. There is exactly one molecule record per unique structure in your workspace.
- A **[batch](/reference/glossary#batch)** is a physical lot of that material — a specific synthesis run, purchase, or receipt. One molecule can have many batches made by different chemists at different times.

When you register a new compound for the first time, chemcellar creates both a molecule record and a first batch record in a single step.

## Steps

### 1. Open the registration form

In the sidebar, click **Molecules**. The molecule list opens.

In the top-right corner of the list, click **Register molecule**.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the Molecules list page with the "Register molecule" button highlighted in the top-right corner.*

The registration dialog opens. It has two sections: **Structure** on the left and **Metadata** on the right.

---

### 2. Enter the structure

You have three ways to provide the structure:

**Option A — Draw it with the structure editor.** The embedded structure editor (Ketcher) opens in the dialog. Use the drawing toolbar on the left to add atoms and bonds. When you are satisfied with the structure, click **Apply** to transfer it to the registration form.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the structure editor (Ketcher) open in the registration dialog, with a benzene ring drawn and the Apply button visible.*

**Option B — Paste a SMILES.** Click the **SMILES** tab above the structure editor and paste your SMILES string into the input. The structure renders instantly in the preview pane.

**Option C — Paste an InChI or InChIKey.** Click the **InChI** tab and paste. chemcellar converts it to a canonical structure for display.

Try the interactive structure viewer below to see how SMILES strings render:

<StructureViewer smiles="CC(=O)Oc1ccccc1C(=O)O" editable={true} />

*Try it: replace the SMILES above (aspirin) with your own compound.*

<Callout type="default" emoji="💡">chemcellar always canonicalizes your structure on registration, following the ChEMBL/PubChem standard pipeline. The InChIKey displayed after you enter a structure is the canonical form — this is what deduplication checks against.</Callout>

---

### 3. Fill in the required metadata

Switch focus to the **Metadata** panel on the right. The only required fields are marked with an asterisk.

| Field | Required? | What to enter |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | Yes | The common or IUPAC name. Can be updated later. |
| **Molecule type** | Yes | Select from: small molecule, peptide, biologic, antibody, nucleic acid, mixture, unknown. |
| **Originating organisation** | Yes | The organisation that first made or owns this compound. Select from the list. Contact your admin if the organisation is not listed. |

<Callout type="info">The **Originating organisation** field tracks IP provenance. It records which organisation (your lab, a CRO partner, or a vendor) first created or disclosed this compound. This is distinct from who is registering it now.</Callout>

---

### 4. Add optional metadata

These fields are optional but strongly recommended for any compound that will go through screening:

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| **External identifiers** | Vendor catalogue numbers, CAS numbers, legacy IDs from other systems. Click **Add identifier**, choose the type, and enter the value. |
| **Lifecycle stage** | Default is `registered`. Change to `active`, `hit`, `lead`, etc. if you already know the compound's stage. |
| **Synthesis status** | `virtual`, `designed`, `synthesized`, or `purchased`. |
| **Tags** | Free-text labels, e.g., "tool compound", "series A", "BBB probe". Separate with Enter. |
| **Custom fields** | Your workspace administrator may have defined additional fields (e.g., "Tumor model"). Fill these in if they appear. |
| **Invention date** | The date the compound was first conceived (for IP purposes). Distinct from today's registration date. |

---

### 5. Add first batch details (optional but recommended)

Below the molecule fields, expand the **First batch** section. Adding batch details now saves a separate step later.

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| **Batch number** | Auto-generated if you leave it blank; or enter your lab notebook batch number. |
| **Amount** | The quantity of material (e.g., 50 mg). |
| **Purity** | Percentage purity (e.g., 97.5%). |
| **Storage location** | Select a location from inventory, or leave blank and assign later. |

---

### 6. Submit the registration

Click **Register** at the bottom of the dialog.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the completed registration form with a structure visible in the editor, name and molecule type filled in, and the Register button highlighted.*

chemcellar runs the registration pipeline:

1. Canonicalizes your structure (strips salts, normalises tautomers, computes the [InChIKey](/reference/glossary#inchikey)).
2. Checks for an existing molecule with the same InChIKey in your workspace.
3. Checks that any external identifiers you entered are not already claimed by another molecule.
4. Creates the molecule record, assigns a permanent **registration number** (e.g., `CV-00001`), and creates the first batch.
5. Computes and stores structural descriptors: molecular weight, logP, [TPSA](/reference/glossary#tpsa), H-bond donors and acceptors.

This happens in under two seconds for a single molecule.

<Callout type="warning">Registration is recorded in the append-only audit trail. The registration number assigned is permanent and will not change, even if you later correct the structure or merge this compound with another. This is by design — it preserves traceability for any data already associated with this record.</Callout>

---

## What happens next

### If no duplicate was found

chemcellar confirms with a success banner: **"CV-00001 registered successfully."** Click **View molecule** to open the detail page.

> 📷 *Screenshot: the success confirmation banner showing the new registration number CV-00001 and a "View molecule" link.*

The molecule detail page shows the structure, all properties, and the first batch listed under the **Batches** tab.

### If the structure already exists

chemcellar tells you: **"This structure matches existing molecule CV-00042."** You have two options:

- **Add a batch to CV-00042** — the normal path. Your batch is added to the existing compound; no duplicate molecule is created.
- **Cancel** — if you believe this is a different compound (stereo isomer, salt form), cancel and review your structure. Contact your administrator if you need to register a related structure as a distinct entity.

### If an identifier conflict is found

chemcellar blocks registration and shows: **"Identifier XYZ-001 is already assigned to molecule CV-00030."** Resolve the conflict by removing or correcting the conflicting identifier before re-submitting.

---

## See the registered molecule

After registration, open the **Molecules** list and search for your registration number or name. Click the row to open the detail page.

The **Overview** tab shows:

- the canonical [SMILES](/reference/glossary#smiles) and InChIKey
- the 2D structure depiction
- computed descriptors (MW, logP, TPSA, HBD, HBA)
- lifecycle stage, synthesis status, and tags

The **Batches** tab lists the first batch you created.

The **History** tab shows the `MoleculeRegistered` event in the audit trail, timestamped and attributed to your user account.

---

## Bulk registration

If you have many compounds to register at once, use the **Bulk registration** feature instead of this form. Go to **Molecules → Bulk import** and upload an SDF or CSV file. chemcellar processes each row independently, deduplicates automatically, and shows a summary of how many molecules were created, how many matched existing records, and how many had errors.
