# Getting started with chemcellar

**Who this is for:** Everyone new to chemcellar — bench chemists, biologists, portfolio managers, and students joining a research team.

**What you'll accomplish:** By the end of this section you'll have logged in, chosen a workspace, oriented yourself in the interface, and registered your first molecule.

---

## What is chemcellar?

chemcellar is a chemical compound management and screening platform designed for drug-discovery teams. It gives every member of a research program — chemists, biologists, project managers — a single, shared record of:

- every [molecule](/reference/glossary#molecule) the team has ever made or acquired,
- every [batch](/reference/glossary#batch) of physical material and where it is stored,
- every assay [protocol](/reference/glossary#protocol) and experimental [run](/reference/glossary#run) performed against those compounds, and
- every [project](/reference/glossary#project) and [collection](/reference/glossary#collection) that organises the work.

Because all data lives in one system, a hit from a screening run automatically links back to the exact compound, batch, and chemist who made it — without any copy-pasting between spreadsheets.

---

## The core workflow

chemcellar follows a linear research loop:

1. **Register** — A chemist draws or imports a structure. chemcellar canonicalizes it, assigns a permanent registration number (e.g., `CV-00001`), and checks for duplicates.
2. **Organize** — Molecules are grouped into projects and collections. Saved searches let you revisit a subset of compounds any time.
3. **Screen** — A biologist runs an assay against a plate of compounds. Results — raw readouts, dose-response curves, quality metrics — are attached to the run and linked back to each molecule.
4. **Search & analyze** — Anyone can search by structure similarity, substructure, property range, or saved filter. Results can be exported to CSV, Excel, or SDF.

Every step is logged in an append-only audit trail. Records that affect regulatory submissions carry electronic signatures under 21 CFR Part 11.

## Before you start

You need:

- An account in the **Sentinel** identity service used by your organisation. Your administrator creates this for you.
- Membership in at least one chemcellar workspace with at least the **viewer** role.
- To register or edit compounds you need the **editor** role or a custom role that includes the `cellar:register` permission.

Ask your workspace administrator if you are unsure which role you have.
